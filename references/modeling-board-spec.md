# Modeling-Board Character Design Reference Specification

Apply this specification to the concrete visual details observed in the user's character image. Write a finished image-generation prompt, not a meta-template.

## Output Contract

Return only:

1. One production-ready English prompt in a clean copyable code block.
2. A two- or three-sentence Korean summary covering the character's identity and outfit, the three-view turnaround, the dominant face close-up, and the six-detail grid.

The English prompt must be 5,000 characters or fewer, counting letters, spaces, punctuation, and line breaks inside the prompt but excluding code-fence markers and the Korean summary. Target approximately 4,500–4,800 characters. If the draft is too long, compress repeated details and generic adjectives while preserving every mandatory layout rule, core identity and outfit evidence, material/detail requirements, consistency block, and essential negative constraint.

Do not add headings, analysis notes, caveats, alternatives, placeholders, or setup instructions outside those two items.

## Required Board Layout

Create a high-definition, clean, minimalist landscape character design board on a pure white background. The presentation should resemble a professional game-art character modeling sheet, fashion design reference page, character design sheet, or character turnaround board. Use generous white space, clearly separated information areas, realistic premium rendering, consistent neutral studio lighting, sharp character edges, clear garment construction, natural hair strands, refined skin, and accurate material response.

Arrange the board as follows:

### Left Main Area: Three-View Turnaround

Place three full-body standing views in the main visual area on the left, arranged in a clean row:

1. Front full-body standing pose.
2. Exact 90-degree left-side full-body standing pose, showing the character's left side in strict profile.
3. Back full-body standing pose.

All three figures must be the exact same character with identical facial features, hairstyle, clothing, body shape, height, proportions, colors, materials, and accessories. Show the complete head and face in all three views. Use natural neutral standing poses with both arms hanging naturally at the sides, no obstruction, eye-level camera, orthographic-style presentation, neutral studio lighting, and no exaggerated perspective. Keep the entire character visible from head to toe without cropping. Do not use a three-quarter angle, torso twist, dynamic pose, or perspective drift in the left-side view.

### Upper-Right Area: One Dominant Facial Close-Up

Place one large front-facing facial close-up of the same character in the upper-right section. It must be a single dominant portrait, not a grid or multiple angles. Show the face clearly at close range with sharp focus on the eyes, nose, mouth, skin texture, hairline, hair parting, and all distinctive facial features. The face, expression, proportions, hairstyle, and accessories must exactly match the three turnaround figures.

### Lower-Right Area: Six Detail Close-Ups

Place six clean close-up detail images in the lower-right section, arranged in a balanced 2-by-3 grid. Each detail must match the main character and outfit exactly. Include:

1. Upper-garment fabric texture and print.
2. Front close-up of the lower-body clothing.
3. Hip, waistband, seam, or tailoring construction detail.
4. Leg, trouser material, or visible skin texture detail.
5. Eyes or another distinctive facial-feature detail.
6. Full close-up of both shoes as a standalone footwear reference, showing their complete shape, materials, fastening, sole, and color placement.

Use the most informative visible crop for each requested detail. Show realistic material texture, stitching, folds, surface response, and construction without inventing prominent elements that are hidden in the reference. The detail images are reference insets, not decorative thumbnails, and must remain visually consistent with the main figures and portrait.

## Character Analysis Content

Translate the source image into precise visual language. Include the apparent identity and age impression, body build, height impression, facial structure, skin tone and texture, hair shape and texture, complete outfit layers, exact color placement, patterns, seams, closures, cuffs, hems, pockets, footwear construction, jewelry, glasses, headwear, and every visible accessory. Preserve asymmetric left/right placement correctly across front, left-side, and back views. For unseen areas, use only the simplest visually consistent continuation. Do not invent prominent new weapons, props, logos, or accessories.

Match the source's realistic, illustrated, stylized, 2D, 3D, or hybrid rendering register. Keep the same identity, proportions, clothing construction, materials, colors, expression, hairstyle, accessories, and rendering quality in the main views, portrait, and all six detail insets.

## Background, Lighting, and Presentation

Use a pure white background with a seamless uncluttered studio presentation, generous white space, balanced spacing, aligned view scales, and clear separation between the left turnaround area and right detail areas. Use consistent eye-level neutral studio lighting, soft grounded contact shadows beneath the standing figures, realistic skin and hair rendering, crisp garment edges, and accurate material response.

Do not place explanatory text, labels, arrows, captions, measurement lines, decorative graphics, logos, watermarks, UI elements, like/save buttons, interface chrome, social-media framing, scenery, furniture, architecture, extra props, or unrelated objects anywhere on the board.

## Consistency and Preservation Block

Strictly maintain identical proportions, identity, and clothing across all views and detail images. The front, exact left-side, and back figures, the dominant facial portrait, and every detail inset must represent the same character at the same apparent age. Preserve the exact facial identity, body shape, height proportions, hairstyle, hairline, clothing layers, garment construction, patterns, materials, colors, footwear, jewelry, glasses, headwear, and accessory placement. Do not mirror asymmetric details incorrectly when rotating the character. Do not redesign, simplify, recolor, remove, replace, add, or stylize away any visible character element. Do not create alternate costumes, different ages, changed body proportions, inconsistent scales, or mismatched detail crops.

## Negative Constraints

End with a compact negative block prohibiting cropped heads, faces, hands, feet, or shoes; incomplete turnaround figures; three-quarter or twisted side views; dynamic poses; inconsistent identities, faces, hairstyles, body shapes, clothing, colors, patterns, materials, accessories, lighting, or rendering; incorrectly mirrored asymmetry; invented props, weapons, logos, labels, or details; duplicate or fused anatomy; distorted hands or footwear; cluttered backgrounds; text, captions, arrows, measurement graphics, decorative borders, watermarks, UI elements, social-media screenshot appearance, and extra objects.
